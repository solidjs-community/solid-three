# Configuring the raycaster

The raycaster is the small piece of machinery that turns "you pressed the
mouse" into "you hit *this* mesh." Most of the time, the defaults are exactly
what you want. Two cases come up where you'll want to tweak it:

- **Points and Lines** are mathematically zero-volume — the default raycaster
  will basically never hit them. You need to give it a tolerance.
- **Layers** let you exclude meshes from raycasting (a HUD overlay, a debug
  gizmo, a particle field) without removing them from the scene.

Both knobs hang off the `raycaster` prop on `<Canvas>`.

## Thresholds for Points and Lines

`<Canvas raycaster={{ params: { Points: { threshold: 0.1 } } }}>` tells the
raycaster "treat points as if they had a 0.1-unit radius." Same idea for
lines.

<Demo code={pointsSnippet} />

Click anywhere near a point. Without the `Points.threshold` setting, you'd
have to land the pointer on a mathematically infinitesimal point — i.e., never.

## Layers

Every `Object3D` carries a `layers` mask. By default the raycaster checks
layer 0 only. Move a mesh to a different layer and it disappears from the
raycaster (but stays visible to the camera).

<Demo code={layersSnippet} />

The grey cube behind sits on layer 1. Click straight through it — only the
front blue cube responds. The grey cube is still rendered; it just isn't a
ray target.

## Beyond defaults

`solid-three` exports two raycaster classes:

- `CursorRaycaster` (the default) — casts from the camera through the
  pointer.
- `CenterRaycaster` — casts from the centre of the canvas, useful for
  VR-style gaze interaction.

Pass an instance via the `raycaster` prop to swap behaviours, or write your
own by extending `Raycaster` and implementing `update(event, context)`. Rare
in practice, but it's there.
